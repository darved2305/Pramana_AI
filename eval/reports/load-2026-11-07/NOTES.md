# Load test, 2026-11-07: three calls for forty minutes

Generated by `uv run python scripts/load_test.py --minutes 40 --out eval/reports/load-2026-11-07`
on `f660b62`. Read `report.md` and `summary.json` for the generated measurements.

**Real:** separate simulator and core processes, a dedicated PostgreSQL database, fresh keys,
participant call sockets, per-question recognizer sessions, second decode, extractor, engine,
approval, capability, repair, verification, reveal and replay. The current voice runner closed
its clients and speaking tasks between investigations. This test measures core and simulator
resident memory, not the driver's memory.

**Emulated:** Gnani's wire protocol only. The clips encode their expected text for the local
emulator. No Gnani service was called and no provider accuracy, latency or capacity was measured.
Browser suites and builds did not run concurrently. The free-tier LLM batch was mostly pacing
provider calls during the first part and finished before the soak ended.

**Supported:** all 160 investigations verified and were correct on reveal; all 160 replays were
byte-identical. There were 1,110 voice answers, 15 withdrawals, zero call errors, and at most
three recognizer sessions at once. All 1,125 sessions were closed at the end; the governor cap
was 16.

**Memory:** core RSS started at 111.6 MB, peaked at 140.7 MB and ended at 135.7 MB. First-half
and second-half means were 133.2 and 132.8 MB. The regression over the second half was
0.11728153328082362 MB/min for core and 0.0255891804391279 MB/min for simulator, both below the
predefined 0.5 MB/min gate. The core's larger initial rise did not continue at that rate over
the longer run. These data support warm-up and bounded caches for this workload; they do not
prove mathematically flat memory or rule out every slow leak.

The engine cache is bounded at 32 investigations, question audio at 64 entries/32 MiB, and
simulator scenarios at 500. This run does not fill the simulator's entire scenario capacity.
It repeats specified S1, not arbitrary faults or unlimited callers, and does not test human
accents, noisy rooms, real-network speech timing or a multi-day workload.
